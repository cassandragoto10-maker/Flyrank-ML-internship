📊 Predicting Page Decline for Content Refresh Prioritization

Lane 2 Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring • March 2026

Author: Cassandra Goto
Date: September 2026

Abstract

This paper presents a decision tree model that predicts which pages are declining in search performance using observable signals: impressions, click-through rate, and average position. Trained on 331,437 pages from March 2026, the model achieves 30-40% Precision@K, identifying pages most likely to benefit from content refresh. The model's interpretable tree structure allows content managers to understand exactly which signals drive decline and prioritize editorial resources on pages with the highest recovery potential. Unlike black-box approaches, this model can be read, challenged, and improved by domain experts.

1. Introduction

Content management teams face a common challenge: thousands of pages exist, but limited editorial resources mean only a fraction can be reviewed and refreshed each month. The key question is: which pages should be reviewed first?

This work supports the decision of which pages to flag for content refresh by ranking pages based on their likelihood of declining. Instead of manually reviewing thousands of pages, this model provides an evidence-based ranking that surfaces pages most likely to benefit from refresh.

Key decision supported:

  • Which pages should be flagged for content review?
  • What is the urgency ranking for each page?
  • What signals drive page decline?

Audience: Content managers, SEO specialists, and editorial teams.

3. Methodology

Assumptions

  • Pages with declining trend direction are candidates for refresh
  • Past 30-day performance is a reliable indicator of current page health
  • Pages with high impressions but low CTR have engagement opportunity

Features

FeatureDescriptionAvailable when?
impressions_90dTotal impressions in March 2026At decision time (past month data)
ctrClick-through rate (clicks/impressions)At decision time (calculated from metrics)
avg_positionAverage search positionAt decision time (observed)

Label Definition

  • Label: is_declining = 1 if trend_pct < -10% (impressions dropped by >10% from first half to second half of March)
  • Why: A 10% drop in impressions indicates meaningful decline worth investigating

Baseline

  • Hand-rule: Pages with high impressions AND low CTR ranked by impressions
  • high_impressions = impressions_90d >= 100
  • low_ctr = CTR < 1%
  • Why: Captures human intuition that "pages getting traffic but not converting need review"

Model

  • Algorithm: Decision Tree (max_depth=2)
  • Why: Interpretable, readable as if/else rules
  • Framework: scikit-learn

Validation Design

  • Split: Train/Test split (70/30) on March 2026 data
  • Metric: Precision@K (K = 10, 20, 50, 100)

Decision Tree Structure

|--- impressions_90d > 0.50
|   |--- ctr <= 0.00
|   |   |--- class: 1 (declining)

What the tree learned:

  • impressions_90d is the dominant predictor (99% importance)
  • Pages with any impressions and CTR ≤ 0 are most likely to decline
  • This makes sense: pages getting traffic but not clicks are candidates for content refresh

5. Limitations

  • Limited features: Only 3 features were available. The original content_refresh table had more.
  • Simple label definition: Declining = >10% drop in impressions. Different thresholds would change results.
  • No time-series validation: A true time-series split would be more rigorous.
  • Imbalanced data: Only 17.6% of pages are declining. Recall wasn't measured.
  • No causality: Correlation between low CTR and decline does not mean low CTR causes decline.

6. Ranked Recommendations

Priority 1: "High Impressions, Zero CTR" Pages

  • Definition: impressions_90d > 100 AND CTR = 0
  • Action: Immediate content review
  • Why: Visible but not engaging. Refresh could capture existing traffic.

Priority 2: "High Impressions, Low CTR" Pages

  • Definition: impressions_90d > 100 AND CTR < 0.5%
  • Action: Content audit and metadata optimization
  • Why: Some engagement exists but below typical benchmarks.

Priority 3: "High Position, Low CTR" Pages

  • Definition: avg_position < 10 AND CTR < 1%
  • Action: Review title tags and meta descriptions
  • Why: Ranking well but not converting.

Recommended Workflow

  1. Export top 100 pages from the model's ranking
  2. Review Priority 1 pages first (highest chance of impact)
  3. Refresh content and track performance over 30 days
  4. Re-run model monthly to update recommendations
